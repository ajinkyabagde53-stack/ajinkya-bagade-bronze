01 · The Brief

Quality over volume, by design.

A Google Ads and Meta Ads take-home assignment, extended into a full portfolio project. I created the brand (ORRA), the creatives and the landing page to show how the whole system would work together.

Product
€30,000+ hand-built electric motorcycle. Premium, low-volume, built to order.
Markets
Western Europe. Germany, France, Belgium, Netherlands as priority markets.
Media budget
€10,000 per month, media spend only.
Primary goal
Qualified test rides and serious buyer and dealer enquiries.
Secondary goal
Build an audience and email database for a 3–6 month consideration cycle.
Not the goal
Maximum lead volume. A full inbox of unqualified form fills is a cost, not a win.

The challenge

01High price

€30,000+ is a considered purchase. Nobody books after one ad.

02Low purchase frequency

Customers buy once in years, so there is no repeat-purchase data to lean on.

033–6 month sales cycle

The sale closes offline, long after the click. Platforms can't see it.

04Limited sales capacity

The team can work ~5–10 qualified leads a week. Volume beyond that is waste.

05Low awareness

A new brand. Few people search for it by name yet.

06Lead quality > lead volume

One serious buyer is worth more than fifty curious clicks.

Not a volume account. The goal is the lowest steady cost of a qualified opportunity the sales team can actually work. The online funnel ends in a booked test ride; the sale happens offline, months later.

Assumptions the plan is built on

Tracking
GA4, Meta Pixel + Conversions API and Google tags in place; the booking form is a tracked event.
CRM
Records Lead → Qualified → Test Ride → Opportunity → Sale, and pushes offline conversions back to the platforms.
Sales capacity
~5–10 qualified leads/week, so volume is capped at ~20–40 qualified leads/month by design.
Sales cycle
3–6 months; few sales close inside 90 days. Q1 is judged on leading indicators.

Every target on this page is an initial planning target to validate in month 1, not an industry benchmark and not a result. Research: Google Keyword Planner, Google Trends.

The 90-day plan

Month 1: Validate
  • Make tracking and conversion definitions trustworthy end to end.
  • Read real search demand and search-term quality in the two lead markets.
  • Test audiences, creative and message fit.
  • Measure landing-page conversion; agree with sales what a qualified lead is.
  • Keep scale low while the data settles.
Months 2–3: Concentrate & scale
  • Switch on the remaining markets once cost per qualified lead is readable.
  • Shift budget to the patterns producing qualified leads.
  • Refine Meta audiences and creative; retire tired ads.
  • Once Search and conversion data are solid, test Demand Gen.
  • Feed qualified-lead and offline data back; scale where quality holds.

Built for the sale, not the form fill.

Deliberately not optimised for lead volume. If the platforms only see form fills, they learn to find people who fill in forms. The account is built to feed qualified-lead signals back from the CRM, so the algorithms learn to find buyers.

04 · Google Ads · €6,000/month Proposed strategy

All budget on intent, first.

Search first: capture the people already looking for an electric motorcycle, then widen the net once conversion data is trustworthy.

Channel roadmap

Non-brand SearchNow

The primary capture engine: category, commercial, performance and competitor terms.

Brand SearchNow

Isolated campaign. Cheap protection of our own name as awareness grows.

Demand Gen / YouTubeMonth 3+

Once the performance film and retargeting pools exist. Builds demand Search can then capture.

Performance MaxLater

Only after offline qualified-lead conversions feed back. Before that, it optimises toward cheap form fills.

ShoppingNot prioritised

No online checkout. Nobody buys a €30,000 motorcycle from a product feed.

The budget reality

€6,000 across four markets and three languages is thin. Split evenly, every campaign gets about €1,500 a month, which is too little data for any of them to learn. So month 1 runs non-brand Search in Germany and the Netherlands only, plus brand everywhere. Belgium and France switch on in month 2, once cost per qualified lead is readable.

Bidding, in plain terms

Non-brand: Maximize Conversions
Start by optimising for leads (test-ride enquiries). They happen often enough to give Google a signal to learn from. As the CRM fills, move to qualified leads.
Target CPA: later
Once qualified-lead data is reliable, test Target CPA to hold a cost per lead. No rigid conversion-count rule; we move when the data is trustworthy.
Brand Search: separate
Kept apart from non-brand, so cheap brand clicks never flatter non-brand performance.
Structure principle
One campaign per market and language keeps budget, language and performance separate.

Creative QA · why the engineering ad has a v2

The first render (left) looked premium but showed a combustion engine, with exhaust headers and cylinder heads. On an electric motorcycle ad that's the one mistake an enthusiast spots instantly. 06 · The Landing Page

One page, one job: the test ride.

Every Google click lands on a page built as ORRA's own site, not as part of this portfolio. It's live and fully interactive. Click into the laptop to scroll it, or open it full-size.

Open live landing page → Desktop and mobile shown side by side because most Search clicks for this category arrive on a phone.

Built to qualify, not just convert

01Message match

The hero CTA repeats the ad's offer, "Book a private test ride", so the click feels like a continuation, not a new page.

02Two-step form

Step 1 asks four things: name, email, country, timeframe. Everything else is optional. Fewer drop-offs from cold search traffic.

03Timeframe as a qualifier

"Within 3 months" vs "just exploring" lets the CRM score and route the lead before sales ever calls.

04Dealers routed separately

Dealers get their own link and form. A dealer and a private buyer are different conversations with different owners.

05Objections on the page

Range and charging get their own section and FAQ, the first doubt any electric motorcycle buyer has.

06Mobile first

A sticky "Book a test ride" bar appears after the hero on phones and hides when the form is on screen.

07Specs in one place

All product figures live in a single config, so concept specs can be swapped for homologated ones without touching the layout.

08Honest by default

The page is noindex, labelled as a concept, and the forms post nowhere. No real person's data is ever collected.

08 · Measurement

What I would measure.

No campaign results are claimed here. This is the measurement system I'd put in place, and the number I'd manage the account to.

Lead→Qualified lead→Test ride→Opportunity→Sale
Primary optimisation metric
Cost per qualified opportunity

It's the earliest number that reflects revenue rather than activity. CPL can look great while sales drowns in tyre-kickers; this metric can't.

Supporting metrics Qualified lead rateTest ride rateCPLCost per qualified leadOpportunity rateCACRevenue / ROAS (later)

For the first 60 days, I would deliberately deprioritise ROAS, because the commercial conversion happens offline and the sales cycle is 3–6 months. Early ROAS is zero or noise. Manage to cost per qualified lead and test-ride bookings; bring ROAS and CAC in once real sales data exists.

09 · Diagnosis Assignment scenario

Three weeks in, something's wrong.

The scenario · hypothetical figures from the brief

The ads are getting clicks (CTR 2.8%) and plenty of traffic, but almost no one finishes the test-ride form (0.2%), cost per lead is about 4× target, and the few leads are low quality. The clicks are fine; something is going wrong after the click. I'd check in this order, quickest first.

Tracking
Check this first. A 0.2% rate is often a broken or double-counted form event, not a real one.
Form / landing page
Is the page slow, hard to use on a phone, or is the form too long? The most common real cause, and the reason the landing page above uses a two-step form.
Traffic quality
Are clicks coming from the right searches, or cheap, curious clicks that were never going to buy?
Message match
Does the landing page deliver what the ad promised, or is there a gap that loses people?
Lead quality
What does sales say: real people but the wrong profile, or the offer attracting the wrong person?
01
Fix tracking & the form first.
Confirm conversions record correctly, then remove anything that makes the form hard to complete. Fastest fix, costs nothing.
02
Move budget toward quality, not cheaper CPL.
Spend where leads actually turn into qualified opportunities, even if those leads cost a little more.
03
Test clearer price & test-ride messaging.
Leading with price and the test ride attracts the right buyer and filters out the wrong one.
How long to wait: tracking and form fixes show within a few days; budget and ad changes need about two to three weeks to read; whether leads become sales takes the full 3–6 month cycle. Quick fixes get quick decisions; quality takes patience.
